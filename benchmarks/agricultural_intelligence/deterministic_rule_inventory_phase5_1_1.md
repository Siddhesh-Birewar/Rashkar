## 1. Executive Summary & Audit Methodology

During Phase 5.1.1, every deterministic rule in `services/api/app/services/agriculture_intelligence/` was audited against the 12 canonical agricultural knowledge sources registered in the production database (Supabase PostgreSQL 17, Alembic head `005_evidence_provenance_updates`).

### Key Evidence Boundaries Enforced:
1. **Pomegranate Soil Depth**: Arbitrary 45–60 cm depth hard disqualification ($< 45\text{ cm} \to \text{disqualified}$) was eliminated. Per ICAR-NRCP & MANAGE 2022 (pp. 9–10), shallow/gravelly soils up to 1 m depth are suitable when planted in $1\text{m} \times 1\text{m} \times 1\text{m}$ pits with topsoil, FYM, and sand/murrum amendments. Depth is strictly an operational diagnostic variable, not a categorical disqualifier.
2. **Chickpea Salinity Risk**: Arbitrary $\text{EC} \ge 1.5\text{ dS/m}$ hard cutoff was eliminated. Per ICRISAT 2010 (p. 8), saline soils and high water table fields are unsuitable. Salinity is enforced as a diagnostic risk trigger that withholds unconditional suitability (`CONDITIONALLY_SUITABLE_PENDING_DATA`) in canal/well command areas until soil EC testing is conducted.
3. **Chickpea Weather-Aware Irrigation**: Automatic $\ge 40\text{ mm}$ in 48h irrigation suspension heuristic was replaced with a conditional weather-aware advisory. In accordance with ICRISAT 2010 (pp. 8, 11) and MPKV 2023 (Rec. 8), scheduled irrigations must be suspended/postponed upon forecast rainfall while evaluating drainage and soil moisture.
4. **Sugarcane Perennial Hydrological Prerequisite**: Grounded sugarcane water constraints in MPKV Package of Practices 2023 (Tier 1: VERIFIED, 2000–3000 mm furrow / 1000–1500 mm drip) rather than unverified bibliographic figures (IISR 2022, 1500–2500 mm). The rule explicitly establishes that seasonal water depletion by February deprives the crop during the critical summer tillering/formative stage (March–May), justifying categorical disqualification due to annual hydrological deficit.

## 3. Detailed Resolutions of Critical Issues

### Issue A: Pomegranate Soil Depth
- **Previous Unverified Rule**: Disqualified pomegranate if effective soil depth was $< 45\text{ cm}$ or $< 60\text{ cm}$.
- **Evidence Audit**: ICAR-NRCP & MANAGE 2022 (pp. 9–10) establishes that pomegranate is cultivated on a wide spectrum of soils across Maharashtra. In soils that are not deep or are rocky/gravelly up to 1 m depth, planting is performed in pits ($1\text{m} \times 1\text{m} \times 1\text{m}$) filled with topsoil, FYM, and sand/murrum (1:1:1). Trench planting ($0.75\text{m} \times 0.75\text{m}$) is reserved for deep soils ($> 1\text{ m}$).
- **Correction Applied**: Removed the arbitrary 45 cm numeric hard constraint. Pomegranate is never disqualified on soil depth alone. When depth is $< 1\text{ m}$, the engine issues a diagnostic advisory detailing pit preparation ($1\text{m} \times 1\text{m} \times 1\text{m}$) and flags shallow depth over bedrock as an aggravating factor for waterlogging in ill-drained soils.

### Issue B: Chickpea Soil Salinity
- **Previous Unverified Rule**: Enforced $\text{EC} < 1.5\text{ dS/m}$ as an automatic numerical cutoff.
- **Evidence Audit**: ICRISAT Chickpea Seed Production Manual 2010 (p. 8) explicitly states: "Saline soil and fields with a high water table are not suitable for chickpea." However, it does not define an empirical $1.5\text{ dS/m}$ cutoff in the text.
- **Correction Applied**: Removed the arbitrary $1.5\text{ dS/m}$ threshold. Any detected salinity is evaluated as an agronomic risk under ICRISAT 2010 (p. 8), advising comprehensive soil testing, water table depth verification, and drainage inspection. In canal/well command areas of Baramati and Indapur, missing EC data prevents unconditional recommendation and holds the farm at `CONDITIONALLY_SUITABLE_PENDING_DATA`.

### Issue C: Chickpea Irrigation Suspension
- **Previous Unverified Rule**: Enforced an automatic irrigation suspension rule when 48h rainfall forecast reached $\ge 40\text{ mm}$.
- **Evidence Audit**: The $40\text{ mm}$ figure was an unverified heuristic not found in ICRISAT 2010 or MPKV 2023. ICRISAT 2010 (pp. 8, 11) notes chickpea is acutely sensitive to water stagnation, poor aeration, and collar rot. MPKV 2023 (Rec. 8) recommends only two canal irrigations for the entire season (20–25 DAS and 60–65 DAS).
- **Correction Applied**: Replaced the automatic $40\text{ mm}$ cutoff with a conditional weather-aware advisory. Whenever precipitation is forecast within 48 hours, the system advises suspending and postponing scheduled irrigation, opening drainage furrows, and assessing root-zone moisture once the rainfall event concludes. If drainage or growth stage are missing from farm context, the engine explicitly requests them.

### Issue D: Sugarcane Water Depletion
- **Previous Unverified Rule**: Disqualified sugarcane based on an annual water requirement of 1500–2500 mm (cited to IISR 2022) and water running dry by February.
- **Evidence Audit**: IISR 2022 is classified as Tier 3 (`UNVERIFIED_BIBLIOGRAPHIC`) in CropNet's registry. MPKV Package of Practices 2023 (Tier 1: VERIFIED, pp. 110–125) establishes the regional scientific standard: Suru sugarcane requires 2000–3000 mm under furrow irrigation or 1000–1500 mm under drip micro-irrigation over 12–18 months. Moisture stress during the summer tillering/formative stage (March–May) causes acute tillering reduction, internode shortening, and crop desiccation.
- **Correction Applied**: Grounded the rule strictly in `mpkv_pop_2023` (Tier 1: VERIFIED) and `mpkv_research_2023` (Rec. 3). A water source that runs dry by February fails prior to the critical summer tillering stage (March–May), resulting in verified hydrological deficit disqualification (`UNSUITABLE_VERIFIED_CONSTRAINT`). Sugarcane rainfed cultivation is also disqualified under MPKV PoP 2023.
